## Voting methods — reading

- [RCV and core support](https://voting-in-the-abstract.medium.com/rcv-and-core-support-e0d1780a9184) (Marcus Ogren) — why "core support = first rankings" is incoherent, and IRV vs. Condorcet/STAR values, via the 2022 Alaska special election. Notes and glossary: [voting-notes/rcv-and-core-support.md](voting-notes/rcv-and-core-support.md)
- Ranked Robin results, explained — why 12 ballots can show "1 win" (now 1.5): Copeland matchup wins vs. ballot counts, the worked BV1550 example (Ann/Bob/Cal), BetterVoting's tie-break ladder, and the 2026-08 live retest (BV1550-R1) with screenshots of the fixed tie UI. Notes: [voting-notes/ranked-robin-results-explained.md](voting-notes/ranked-robin-results-explained.md)
- [Ranked Robin's origin thread](https://www.votingtheory.org/forum/topic/136/new-simple-condorcet-method-basically-copeland-margins) (votingtheory.org, 2021) — the method debuted as "Ranked Advantage Voting" and was renamed mid-thread; the first VSE numbers, the ballot-language evolution, and the 3-cycle/5-cycle showcase examples transcribed from PNGs, with errata: both images fail a zero-sum sanity check (winners survive correction). Notes: [voting-notes/ranked-robin-origins.md](voting-notes/ranked-robin-origins.md)
- The origin thread's claims, checked — the four assertions in topic 136 nobody ever ran numbers against. Sass's "adding a weak candidate changes nothing" is a theorem for candidates who lose *every* matchup and false otherwise: a candidate with 2% of first preferences flips the winner, and a six-ballot election is decided by a candidate who wins nothing. "Sum all margins = Borda" is an exact identity that survives equal ranks and truncation, so the Copeland gate is the only thing between Ranked Robin and Borda. The "best average rank" one-liner elects the other candidate under either of two conventions it never states. Ties do thin out with electorate size — but Copeland's own rate stays flat at ~17%, and with ≤4 candidates "no Condorcet winner" and "Copeland tie" are provably the same event, so the margins rung is the entire cycle path in normal-sized fields. Notes: [voting-notes/ranked-robin-thread-claims-checked.md](voting-notes/ranked-robin-thread-claims-checked.md), verifier: [voting-notes/code/thread136-claims/verify.py](voting-notes/code/thread136-claims/verify.py)
- Running the missing VSE — Ranked Robin implemented in Huang's votesim (its `copeland()` had never been run) and benchmarked over 18,000 spatial elections, verified against an independent reference: tops a statistically indistinguishable Condorcet cluster, +3.6 points over honest IRV, and its margins ladder is VSE-neutral but halves unresolved ties — ties are 95% clone dead heats, not cycles, so the famous 1st-Degree tiebreaker almost never fires. Notes: [voting-notes/ranked-robin-vse-run.md](voting-notes/ranked-robin-vse-run.md)
- [Ranked-ballot voting methods](https://www.cs.angelo.edu/~rlegrand/rbvote/) (Rob LeGrand, Angelo State) — 16 methods with worked examples, a 13×12 criterion-compliance table, and a calculator. Its 921-voter example yields four different winners, and the methods can't agree whether the Condorcet loser is the best candidate or the worst. Notes: [voting-notes/legrand-ranked-ballot-methods.md](voting-notes/legrand-ranked-ballot-methods.md)
- Reverse symmetry in the calculator — what the "Reverse all rankings" checkbox is for, and four constructed ballot sets that break it: a 9-voter profile where six methods (Hare, Coombs, Baldwin, Carey, Nanson, Raynaud) elect the same candidate forwards and reversed, one where Hare does it with a Condorcet winner on the ballot, a 5-voter Bucklin failure, and a Simpson/Dodgson pair. All nine of LeGrand's ❌ methods convicted, no tiebreaker involved. Notes: [voting-notes/reverse-symmetry-examples.md](voting-notes/reverse-symmetry-examples.md)
- Center squeeze in the calculator — the famous 99-voter Hare example (relabelled; its original candidate names do the arguing for it), why it's only half a good example, and the neutrally-named 5-candidate one on the same page that beats it: there the Condorcet winner *leads* on first preferences and Hare still eliminates him. Notes: [voting-notes/hare-center-squeeze-examples.md](voting-notes/hare-center-squeeze-examples.md)
- [Putting It Together: Voting Theory](https://courses.lumenlearning.com/wmopen-mathforliberalarts/chapter/putting-it-together-voting-theory/) (Lumen Learning, gen-ed math) — a center squeeze found in the wild, in a textbook that doesn't notice: its own 75-ballot recap exercise eliminates the Condorcet winner by *one vote* under IRV, and also turns out to be non-monotonic (promoting the winner on 6–13 ballots defeats him, handing those voters their last choice), to reward favorite betrayal by the Condorcet winner's own supporters, and to contain two spoilers. Ranked Robin elects Garcia at the wins step while its margins step would say Lee — the two halves of the ladder disagreeing. The module's approval voting has no determinate answer here at all. Notes: [voting-notes/lumen-75-ballot-four-winners.md](voting-notes/lumen-75-ballot-four-winners.md), verifier: [voting-notes/code/lumen-75-ballot/verify.py](voting-notes/code/lumen-75-ballot/verify.py)
- [Approval voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approval_voting) (Wikipedia) — the simplest cardinal method, and the only one whose criterion compliance is a property of *how voters set their cutoff* rather than of the tabulation: four rows in the compliance table, one per voter model, and only fully dichotomous preferences pass everything. Working the Tennessee zero-info scenario by hand elects the Condorcet *loser* on minority approval; the dichotomous-cutoff example isolates the IIA failure to the cutoff rule alone. Keeps the retreats advocacy pages omit — IEEE 2002, Dartmouth 82–18 in 2009, the Independent Party of Oregon leaving for STAR in 2020, North Dakota's April 2025 ban ending Fargo — because universal bullet voting collapses approval into plurality, and 79% bullet voted even in the 1987 MAA election run by the mathematicians who chose the method. Notes: [voting-notes/approval-voting.md](voting-notes/approval-voting.md)
- [STAR voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STAR_voting) (Wikipedia) — a thin, advocacy-sourced article carrying a third-party-sources banner, whose criterion failures are asserted with no examples, so they're built and checked locally instead. One 100-voter profile fails majority, Condorcet and clone independence at once: the strict favourite of 52 of 100, and the Condorcet winner, never reaches the runoff — and deleting the front-runner's clone flips the winner back. Plus a later-no-harm failure where scoring a second choice 4 instead of 0 costs that bloc its own favourite, and a favorite-betrayal failure where exhaustive search over all 216 ballots proves equal-rating won't save you. mono-raise survives 196,699 random profiles; Woodall's mono-raise-delete doesn't. Two arithmetic slips found in the article itself. Notes: [voting-notes/star-voting.md](voting-notes/star-voting.md), verifier: [voting-notes/code/star-voting/verify.py](voting-notes/code/star-voting/verify.py)
- Our strategy pages vs. [Wikipedia's "Strategic voting"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_voting#Cardinal_single-winner_voting) — an audit of the starvoting.org/equal.vote strategy education chain against the encyclopedia article, with draft replacement copy. The arguments hold; the vocabulary, the sourcing, and two of the claims don't. Our four "insincerity" categories match nothing in the literature (nobody searching "burial" finds a heading called *Weak Insincerity*), "Peer Reviewed studies" are invoked twice and named zero times while equal.vote/peer_review names two real journal papers, and "STAR never encourages strong insincerity" is refuted by the exhaustive-search counterexample in our own star-voting.md — where Farewell to Pass/Fail is the answer we already have and don't deploy. Two further verified counterexamples: STAR fails the participation criterion, and at the score cut burial and boosting are the *same move* — a 7-voter profile where a strategist who already has the frontrunner at 0 lifts a third candidate over them instead, and wins, because the "your favorite gets squeezed out" rebuttal only binds when your favorite is marginal. Notes: [voting-notes/star-strategy-pages-vs-wikipedia.md](voting-notes/star-strategy-pages-vs-wikipedia.md), verifier: [voting-notes/code/star-strategy/verify.py](voting-notes/code/star-strategy/verify.py)
