## Voting methods — reading

- [RCV and core support](https://voting-in-the-abstract.medium.com/rcv-and-core-support-e0d1780a9184) (Marcus Ogren) — why "core support = first rankings" is incoherent, and IRV vs. Condorcet/STAR values, via the 2022 Alaska special election. Notes and glossary: [voting-notes/rcv-and-core-support.md](voting-notes/rcv-and-core-support.md)
- Ranked Robin results, explained — why 12 ballots can show "1 win" (now 1.5): Copeland matchup wins vs. ballot counts, the worked BV1550 example (Ann/Bob/Cal), BetterVoting's tie-break ladder, and the 2026-08 live retest (BV1550-R1) with screenshots of the fixed tie UI. Notes: [voting-notes/ranked-robin-results-explained.md](voting-notes/ranked-robin-results-explained.md)
- [Ranked Robin's origin thread](https://www.votingtheory.org/forum/topic/136/new-simple-condorcet-method-basically-copeland-margins) (votingtheory.org, 2021) — the method debuted as "Ranked Advantage Voting" and was renamed mid-thread; the first VSE numbers, the ballot-language evolution, and the 3-cycle/5-cycle showcase examples transcribed from PNGs, with errata: both images fail a zero-sum sanity check (winners survive correction). Notes: [voting-notes/ranked-robin-origins.md](voting-notes/ranked-robin-origins.md)
- Running the missing VSE — Ranked Robin implemented in Huang's votesim (its `copeland()` had never been run) and benchmarked over 18,000 spatial elections, verified against an independent reference: tops a statistically indistinguishable Condorcet cluster, +3.6 points over honest IRV, and its margins ladder is VSE-neutral but halves unresolved ties — ties are 95% clone dead heats, not cycles, so the famous 1st-Degree tiebreaker almost never fires. Notes: [voting-notes/ranked-robin-vse-run.md](voting-notes/ranked-robin-vse-run.md)
- [Ranked-ballot voting methods](https://www.cs.angelo.edu/~rlegrand/rbvote/) (Rob LeGrand, Angelo State) — 16 methods with worked examples, a 13×12 criterion-compliance table, and a calculator. Its 921-voter example yields four different winners, and the methods can't agree whether the Condorcet loser is the best candidate or the worst. Notes: [voting-notes/legrand-ranked-ballot-methods.md](voting-notes/legrand-ranked-ballot-methods.md)
- Reverse symmetry in the calculator — what the "Reverse all rankings" checkbox is for, and four constructed ballot sets that break it: a 9-voter profile where six methods (Hare, Coombs, Baldwin, Carey, Nanson, Raynaud) elect the same candidate forwards and reversed, one where Hare does it with a Condorcet winner on the ballot, a 5-voter Bucklin failure, and a Simpson/Dodgson pair. All nine of LeGrand's ❌ methods convicted, no tiebreaker involved. Notes: [voting-notes/reverse-symmetry-examples.md](voting-notes/reverse-symmetry-examples.md)
- Center squeeze in the calculator — the famous 99-voter Hare example (relabelled; its original candidate names do the arguing for it), why it's only half a good example, and the neutrally-named 5-candidate one on the same page that beats it: there the Condorcet winner *leads* on first preferences and Hare still eliminates him. Notes: [voting-notes/hare-center-squeeze-examples.md](voting-notes/hare-center-squeeze-examples.md)
